## 1. What Is a High-Risk Pregnancy?

A pregnancy is called **high-risk** when certain factors could affect the health of the mother, the baby or both. Early diagnosis and regular, close care help reduce complications.

---

## 2. Causes & Risk Factors

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Mother's age** | Over 35 |
| **Existing health conditions** | Diabetes, high blood pressure, thyroid disorders, heart disease |
| **Multiple pregnancy** | Twins, triplets or more |
| **Past pregnancy history** | Miscarriage, preterm labour, stillbirth |
| **Lifestyle** | Smoking, alcohol, obesity |
| **Pregnancy-related conditions** | Preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, placenta previa |
| **Baby-related findings** | Genetic or structural abnormalities found on screening |

---

## 3. 🚨 Warning Signs — Get Medical Help Immediately

- High blood pressure that doesn't come down
- Severe swelling of the hands, feet or face
- Unexplained vaginal bleeding
- Baby moving less, or not at all
- Strong or continuous abdominal pain
- Severe headache, blurred vision or sudden weight gain

## 8. FAQs

**What are the signs of a high-risk pregnancy?**
See the warning signs above. Seek medical care right away if any of them occur.

**Can I have a normal delivery if my pregnancy is high-risk?**
Often, yes. It depends on the baby's position, where the placenta is, your health and how labour progresses. Conditions like placenta previa, severe preeclampsia or fetal distress may need a C-section. Induction may be advised at a certain stage.

**How often will I need check-ups?**

| Stage | Visit frequency |
|---|---|
| Early pregnancy | Monthly |
| By the 2nd trimester | Every 2 weeks |
| Final weeks | Weekly |

Visits may include an ultrasound, NST and blood tests.

**Are medicines safe during a high-risk pregnancy?**
Some medicines (for example, for blood pressure, gestational diabetes or preventing preterm labour)
